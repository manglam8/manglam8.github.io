Research
Projects
Winter 2026 · Advanced Computer Vision

Search-and-Rescue via Cost-Oriented POMDP

York University · Prof. John K. Tsotsos
  • The question: when should an autonomous agent look at something versus physically interact with it? Both cost time and energy. Framed this as a POMDP where the agent maintains a belief distribution over possible target locations and picks actions based on cost vs. expected information gain.
  • What made it interesting was the cost asymmetry — looking is cheap but slow, acting is faster but commits you. The formal framework forced me to make that tradeoff explicit.
Winter 2026 · Fairness & Bias in AI

Fairness Analysis & Debiasing — CCHS Dataset

York University · Prof. Laleh Seyyed-Kalantari
  • Used the Canadian Community Health Survey 2022 to ask whether a mental health screening model treats indigenous and non-indigenous populations the same, immigrant vs. non-immigrant, people with disabilities vs. without. Short answer: it doesn't, and the gap varies a lot by which metric you use.
  • Tried two fixes — SMOTE resampling and threshold optimization. Neither is obviously better; they trade accuracy for fairness differently depending on the group. That tension doesn't resolve neatly.
Winter 2026 · Fairness & Bias in AI

Neural Network Debiasing — Adult Income Data

York University · Prof. Laleh Seyyed-Kalantari
  • Income prediction on the UCI Adult dataset sounds boring until you measure who the false negatives fall on. Across gender and race, the gaps in TPR were larger than I expected from a "standard" ML pipeline.
  • Also presented InterFair (Majumder et al., EMNLP 2023) to the class — the idea of using natural language feedback to steer a model away from biased outputs is genuinely clever, though it raises questions about who writes the feedback.
Winter 2026 · EXINES Lab

SWE-bench Multimodal — Agent Evaluation

York University · Prof. Maleknaz Nayebi
  • SWE-bench Multimodal extends the original benchmark by adding screenshots, diagrams, and visual context to real GitHub issues. The question we're poking at: do agents actually use the images, or do they mostly solve from the text?
  • Running harness delta tests to compare agent behaviour across benchmark configs. The answer so far is more nuanced than "agents ignore images."
